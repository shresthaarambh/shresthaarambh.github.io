An exchange is a rulebook and a system for bringing buyers and sellers together.

This guide starts with the basic job of an exchange: organize price discovery, apply published trading rules, and record what happened when two sides agree to trade.

What a venue coordinates

  • What products can trade and when the market is open
  • How orders enter, queue, match, amend, or cancel
  • How trades and market data are reported to participants

An exchange is one layer in a larger market structure.

Investors, brokers, market makers, clearing members, custodians, and regulators all play different roles. The exchange runs the trading venue; it does not perform every function around a trade.

VenueRules & matching

Operates the trading schedule, order book, market data, and matching rules.

ParticipantsOrders & liquidity

Investors and market makers contribute trading interest through approved access.

Post-tradeClearing & settlement

Separate infrastructure manages obligations after execution.

Matching logic turns eligible orders into trades.

Many electronic markets use price-time priority: better prices are considered first, and orders at the same price are generally prioritized by their arrival sequence.

Trading technology has to be fast, deterministic, and recoverable.

Order gateways, risk checks, matching engines, market-data distribution, monitoring, and replay capabilities work together. Low latency matters, but a venue must also reconstruct and explain events.

Rules and controls support orderly markets before, during, and after trading.

The exact framework changes by asset class and jurisdiction. This draft will later be refined with the relevant regulatory detail, but the operating pattern is useful now.

Pre-trade controls

Halt procedures, circuit breakers, and clearly defined market states help manage disorderly conditions.

A trade begins upstream and continues downstream from the exchange.

The matching engine is the center of the execution event, not the whole lifecycle. This is the frame for exploring how trading systems connect.
